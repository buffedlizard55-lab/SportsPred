How a prediction is made

Six steps, all of them inspectable.

The pipeline

1 · Collect. The day's fixtures for every verified competition are pulled from ESPN's public key-less scoreboard endpoint. One request per competition, cached in the browser with a time-to-live that depends on whether the day is finished, live or scheduled.

2 · Measure the league. Home-win rate, draw rate and mean combined score are measured from the league's own completed matches. This is the baseline the model starts from, and it is the reason no home-advantage constant appears anywhere in the code. A league with fewer than ten completed matches gets no baseline at all, and every prediction in it is capped below the HIGH band.

3 · Score the signals. Recent form, season record, curated ranking, head-to-head and rest are each turned into a log-odds adjustment. Every one records its rule id, the value that triggered it, the points it contributed and the endpoint it came from. A signal with no data contributes nothing and is written into the match's not available list.

4 · Blend with the market. Where ESPN republishes a price, the three-way (or two-way) implied probabilities are de-vigged proportionally and blended with the model. Where no price exists, the price rules are skipped and the confidence ceiling drops — they are never guessed.

5 · Cap and band. Confidence is the probability plus evidence completeness plus a small market agreement term, then clipped by a cap derived from what was missing.

6 · Write and validate. The tip is assembled only from signals that actually fired, then run through the output validator. A tip that fails any rule is withheld and the violation is displayed instead.

Confidence bands

Ceilings that apply before banding:

  • no measured league baseline → capped at 62
  • no published price → capped at 74
  • fewer than three signals → capped at 66
  • fewer than two signals → not published at all

Output rules

  • the selection must be bolded inside the opening sentence
  • 55–170 words
  • the bolded text must equal the scored selection
  • no guarantee language
  • a SKIP market can never be written up
